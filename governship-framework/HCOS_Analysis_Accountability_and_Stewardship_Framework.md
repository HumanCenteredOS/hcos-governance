# HCOS™ Analysis, Accountability, and Stewardship Framework

**Document Type:** HCOS™ Framework

**Framework:** HCOS™ Governship

**Status:** Version 1.0 — Draft

**Purpose:** To provide a structured method for understanding events, assigning responsibility appropriately, and guiding continuous system improvement.

---

# Purpose

Healthy human systems distinguish between understanding what happened, determining responsibility, and improving the future.

These questions are related, but they are not the same.

When they become confused, organizations often default to blame rather than learning.

The HCOS™ Analysis, Accountability, and Stewardship Framework separates these functions so that organizations can respond with greater clarity, fairness, and long-term effectiveness.

---

# Three Complementary Questions

Every significant decision, event, success, failure, incident, or organizational challenge should be examined through three complementary lenses.

## Analysis

**Question**

> Why did this happen?

Analysis seeks understanding.

It examines:

- system conditions
- workflows
- policies
- incentives
- technology
- resources
- human factors
- communication
- environment
- uncertainty

The purpose of analysis is explanation—not blame.

---

## Accountability

**Question**

> Who is responsible for what happens next?

Accountability assigns responsibility according to:

- authority
- role
- knowledge
- available resources
- decision rights
- actual capacity to influence the outcome

Healthy accountability avoids assigning responsibility where authority or capacity did not exist.

---

## Stewardship

**Question**

> How should the system improve so healthier outcomes become more likely?

Stewardship translates learning into action.

It asks:

- What should change?
- What should remain?
- What should be monitored?
- What should be preserved?
- What should be retired?
- What should future leaders inherit?

Stewardship looks beyond today's outcome toward tomorrow's system.

---

# Why These Questions Matter

Organizations frequently collapse these three questions into one.

Instead of asking:

> Why did this happen?

they ask:

> Whose fault is this?

This shortcut often discourages learning, reduces psychological safety, and overlooks important system factors.

Separating analysis, accountability, and stewardship helps organizations remain both compassionate and responsible.

---

# Guiding Principle

Healthy systems understand before they judge.

They assign accountability fairly.

They steward improvement intentionally.

These responsibilities should strengthen one another rather than compete.
