# Purpose

The HCOS™ Governship Maturity Model provides a structured framework for evaluating how effectively governance and stewardship are practiced within an organization, discipline, project, or the HCOS ecosystem itself.

The model is intended to encourage continuous learning rather than serve as a compliance scorecard.

Higher maturity does not imply perfection.

Instead, maturity reflects an organization's growing ability to exercise authority responsibly while preserving long-term stewardship.

---

# Guiding Principle

Healthy governship is not measured by the amount of control an organization exercises.

It is measured by its ability to steward people, knowledge, and systems responsibly over time.

---

# Characteristics of Mature Governship

As governship matures, organizations increasingly demonstrate:

- clarity of purpose
- appropriate authority
- transparent decision-making
- responsible accountability
- continuous learning
- preservation of institutional knowledge
- adaptability
- protection of human dignity
- long-term stewardship

---

# Level 1 — Informal

## Description

Governance depends primarily upon individuals rather than systems.

Processes are inconsistent, undocumented, or dependent upon institutional memory.

Success often relies upon experienced people rather than shared practices.

## Characteristics

- undocumented decisions
- unclear authority
- inconsistent review
- limited documentation
- reactive decision-making
- minimal version control

## Risks

- loss of institutional knowledge
- inconsistent decisions
- duplication of effort
- dependence upon key individuals
- reduced transparency

## Goal for Advancement

Begin documenting governance practices and establish common terminology.

---

# Level 2 — Defined

## Description

Governance processes have been documented.

Roles, responsibilities, review expectations, and document classifications are becoming standardized.

The organization begins developing common governance language.

## Characteristics

- documented governance framework
- defined review process
- version control
- repository organization
- documented roles
- status labels
- change logs

## Risks

- inconsistent application
- governance exists on paper more than in practice
- variable participation

## Goal for Advancement

Apply governance consistently across projects and repositories.

---

# Level 3 — Operationalized

## Description

Governship practices are routinely applied.

Governance supports everyday decision-making rather than existing only as documentation.

Contributors understand how governance works and use it consistently.

## Characteristics

- regular reviews
- consistent release management
- transparent decision logs
- structured validation
- architectural consistency
- documented accountability
- governance integrated into workflows

## Risks

- process fatigue
- excessive documentation
- governance becoming procedural rather than purposeful

## Goal for Advancement

Develop systematic learning from implementation experience.

---

# Level 4 — Learning

## Description

Governance continuously improves through evidence, implementation experience, and reflection.

Organizations actively learn from both successes and failures.

Feedback becomes an expected part of governship.

## Characteristics

- continuous improvement
- evidence-informed revisions
- structured feedback
- implementation monitoring
- review of unintended consequences
- periodic governance evaluation
- adaptation based on learning

## Risks

- changing too frequently
- overreacting to isolated events
- unnecessary complexity

## Goal for Advancement

Strengthen long-term stewardship while preserving organizational memory.

---

# Level 5 — Stewarded

## Description

Governship has become part of organizational culture.

Governance protects today's integrity while stewardship intentionally prepares future generations to inherit healthy systems.

Knowledge is preserved, relationships remain strong, and adaptation occurs without losing organizational purpose.

## Characteristics

- governance supports human flourishing
- institutional memory preserved
- transparent succession
- resilient knowledge systems
- healthy contributor community
- trusted governance
- responsible innovation
- sustainable stewardship

## Outcomes

Organizations operating at this level demonstrate:

- trustworthy governance
- adaptable systems
- responsible authority
- healthy learning culture
- resilient knowledge management
- long-term continuity
- meaningful human participation

---

# Maturity Is Not Linear

Organizations may demonstrate different maturity levels across different governance domains.

For example:

| Domain | Maturity |
|---------|----------|
| Version Control | Level 4 |
| Repository Management | Level 3 |
| Validation | Level 2 |
| Community Participation | Level 1 |

Improvement should therefore focus on strengthening individual domains rather than achieving a single overall score.

---

# Continuous Improvement Questions

Organizations should periodically ask:

- Are our governance practices still serving people?
- What have we learned since the last review?
- What knowledge is at risk of being lost?
- Are responsibilities aligned with authority?
- Are contributors able to participate meaningfully?
- Have our processes become unnecessarily burdensome?
- What should future leaders inherit?
- Where should stewardship be strengthened?

---

# Relationship to the HCOS™ Governship Framework

This maturity model expands the five governship maturity levels introduced within the HCOS™ Governship Framework into a practical assessment and continuous improvement resource.

It supports implementation of HCOS governance principles while encouraging responsible organizational learning.

---

# Intended Uses

The HCOS™ Governship Maturity Model may be used to:

- evaluate governance practices
- guide organizational improvement
- assess new initiatives
- support leadership development
- evaluate AI governance
- strengthen healthcare governance
- review repository stewardship
- identify governance priorities
- facilitate strategic planning

---

# Guiding Principle

Healthy governship is not the destination.

It is the ongoing practice of protecting purpose, preserving knowledge, supporting people, and improving responsibly so that future generations inherit systems worthy of their trust.
