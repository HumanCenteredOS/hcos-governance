# Purpose

The HCOS™ Governship Principles establish the enduring values that guide governance, stewardship, and decision-making throughout the HCOS ecosystem.

These principles inform every HCOS framework, standard, method, model, instrument, implementation, and governance activity.

They are intended to remain relatively stable over time while allowing the framework itself to continue learning and evolving.

---

# Guiding Principle

Healthy governship protects both institutional integrity and human purpose.

---

# Principle 1 — Human Purpose

Every decision should serve a clear human purpose.

Technology, efficiency, organizational growth, and financial performance should support people rather than replace the purpose of serving them.

---

# Principle 2 — Human Dignity

Every person possesses inherent worth.

Governship should preserve dignity regardless of role, status, productivity, authority, or institutional position.

---

# Principle 3 — Truth and Intellectual Honesty

Clearly distinguish:

- evidence
- interpretation
- professional judgment
- assumptions
- aspirations
- uncertainty

Claims should not exceed available evidence.

---

# Principle 4 — Authority with Accountability

Authority should always be accompanied by transparency, responsibility, and meaningful accountability.

No consequential authority should exist without a clear pathway for review.

---

# Principle 5 — Responsibility with Adequate Authority

People should not be held responsible for outcomes they lacked the authority, information, resources, competence, or psychological safety to influence.

---

# Principle 6 — Transparency

Meaningful decisions should be understandable.

Governship should preserve:

- rationale
- evidence
- assumptions
- uncertainty
- decision history
- change history

---

# Principle 7 — Participation

People affected by decisions should have meaningful opportunities to inform them.

Lived experience is an important source of knowledge.

---

# Principle 8 — Proportionality

Governance activities should be proportional to:

- potential harm
- complexity
- uncertainty
- reversibility
- public impact

Governance should avoid unnecessary bureaucracy.

---

# Principle 9 — Continuous Learning

Healthy systems learn continuously.

Revision represents responsible improvement rather than institutional weakness.

---

# Principle 10 — Stewardship

Governship preserves knowledge, relationships, purpose, and institutional memory so future generations inherit healthier systems.

---

# Principle 11 — Process Integrity

Protective processes should themselves be protected from misuse.

Healthy governship continually evaluates opportunities for coercion, fraud, retaliation, manipulation, and abuse.

---

# Principle 12 — Human Flourishing

The ultimate purpose of governship is to help people, organizations, and communities flourish while preserving dignity, learning, responsibility, and hope.
