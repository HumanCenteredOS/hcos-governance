# Purpose

The HCOS™ Decision Guide provides a structured approach for evaluating significant decisions, events, incidents, proposals, and organizational changes.

Rather than focusing immediately on blame or solutions, the guide separates three complementary functions:

- Analysis
- Accountability
- Stewardship

Separating these functions encourages thoughtful decision-making, supports continuous learning, and helps organizations improve responsibly over time.

This guide is intended to complement the HCOS™ Governship Framework and may be applied across all HCOS disciplines.

---

# Guiding Principle

> Healthy systems seek understanding before judgment, assign responsibility fairly, and steward improvement intentionally.

# The HCOS Decision Process

```text
Recognize
      ↓
Understand
      ↓
Analyze
      ↓
Determine Accountability
      ↓
Develop Stewardship Actions
      ↓
Implement
      ↓
Monitor
      ↓
Learn
      ↓
Improve
```

---

# Step 1 — Recognize

## Question

What requires a decision?

Examples include:

- organizational decisions
- policy changes
- AI implementation
- incidents
- operational failures
- successful innovations
- strategic planning
- financial commitments
- governance questions

Before acting, clearly define what is being evaluated.

---

# Step 2 — Understand

## Questions

What is happening?

Why does it matter?

Who is affected?

Clarify:

- the human purpose
- desired outcome
- people affected
- organizational context
- system boundaries

Understanding should precede analysis.

---

# Step 3 — Analysis

## Primary Question

> Why did this happen?

Analysis seeks understanding rather than blame.

Consider:

### System Factors

- workflows
- policies
- resources
- incentives
- technology
- organizational structures

### Human Factors

- communication
- workload
- knowledge
- training
- fatigue
- cognitive burden

### Environmental Factors

- timing
- competing priorities
- external pressures
- uncertainty

### Evidence

Identify:

- known facts
- assumptions
- interpretations
- remaining uncertainty

The objective is to explain the system, not assign fault.

---

# Step 4 — Accountability

## Primary Question

> Who is responsible for what happens next?

Accountability should reflect:

- actual authority
- decision rights
- available information
- available resources
- professional responsibilities
- capacity to influence outcomes

Avoid assigning responsibility where meaningful authority did not exist.

---

# Step 5 — Stewardship

## Primary Question

> How should the system improve?

Stewardship focuses on healthier future outcomes.

Potential actions include:

- policy revision
- workflow redesign
- additional education
- resource allocation
- technology improvements
- governance changes
- monitoring
- research
- retirement of outdated practices

Stewardship extends beyond correcting individual events.

It strengthens the system itself.

---

# Step 6 — Implementation

Implement agreed-upon actions while ensuring:

- communication
- role clarity
- resources
- training
- documentation
- stakeholder awareness

---

# Step 7 — Monitoring

Determine whether improvements are producing the intended outcomes.

Possible indicators include:

- safety
- quality
- user experience
- workload
- operational performance
- unintended consequences
- emerging risks

---

# Step 8 — Learning

Every significant decision should contribute to organizational learning.

Ask:

- What did we learn?
- What surprised us?
- What should we preserve?
- What should change?
- What new questions emerged?
