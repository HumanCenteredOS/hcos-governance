# HCOS™ Capacity Return Scale

## Human-Centered Evaluation of Whether an Intervention Actually Reduces Burden

**Framework:** Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)
**Domain:** Social Suffering & Human Vulnerability Stewardship
**Type:** Evaluation and Outcome Measurement Tool
**Version:** 1.0 Draft

---

## 1. Purpose

Systems often measure whether help was offered.

They measure:

* resources displayed
* referrals made
* links clicked
* forms completed
* calls placed
* cases closed
* services delivered

But these measures do not necessarily answer the most important question:

## Did anything actually become easier for the human?

The HCOS™ Capacity Return Scale evaluates whether an intervention meaningfully reduces human burden and restores the person's ability to:

* function
* decide
* work
* care
* connect
* recover
* participate
* remain safe
* continue forward

The scale shifts evaluation from:

**service delivery**

to

**human capacity restored**

---

# 2. Core Concept

## Capacity Return

Capacity Return is the amount of usable human capacity restored when a system removes, reduces, shares, or helps resolve a burden.

Capacity may include:

* time
* attention
* cognitive capacity
* emotional capacity
* physical energy
* financial capacity
* caregiving capacity
* relational capacity
* work capacity
* decision-making capacity
* safety
* agency
* hope
* ability to participate in ordinary life

---

# 3. Why Capacity Return Matters

Two interventions can appear identical from the system's perspective while producing very different outcomes.

Example:

A person receives a housing-assistance link.

### Outcome A

The program is closed.

**Capacity Return: None**

### Outcome B

The person learns exactly what documentation is required.

**Capacity Return: Informational**

### Outcome C

The person qualifies and receives an appointment.

**Capacity Return: Access**

### Outcome D

Rent assistance prevents eviction.

**Capacity Return: Functional**

### Outcome E

Housing remains stable long enough for the person to begin employment and rebuild financial stability.

**Capacity Return: Sustained**

The resource itself did not determine success.

The human outcome did.

---

# 4. Capacity Return Scale

## Level 0 — No Capacity Return

### Definition

The intervention does not meaningfully reduce burden.

Examples:

* unusable resource provided
* person does not qualify
* waiting list exceeds the urgency of the need
* information duplicates what the person already knows
* referral creates additional administrative work
* problem remains unchanged

### Human Condition

**Burden remains essentially the same.**

---

## Level 1 — Informational Return

### Definition

The person gains useful understanding, clarity, or orientation.

Examples:

* understands available options
* knows who is responsible
* understands eligibility
* receives accurate instructions
* uncertainty decreases
* confusing information becomes organized

### Human Condition

**The person knows more but still carries most of the burden.**

---

## Level 2 — Access Return

### Definition

The person gains meaningful access to a resource, service, person, or pathway.

Examples:

* appointment scheduled
* navigator assigned
* application accepted
* transportation program becomes available
* legal aid agrees to review the case
* financial assistance application moves forward

### Human Condition

**The path to help becomes usable.**

---

## Level 3 — Functional Return

### Definition

A concrete burden is removed, reduced, transferred, or resolved.

Examples:

* transportation provided
* rent paid
* meals delivered
* childcare secured
* medication obtained
* paperwork completed by a navigator
* utility shutoff prevented
* insurance issue resolved

### Human Condition

**Something materially becomes easier.**

---

## Level 4 — Sustained Capacity Return

### Definition

The intervention restores enough capacity for the person to function more sustainably over time.

Examples:

* stable housing supports continued employment
* reliable childcare allows a parent to remain at work
* transportation consistently supports treatment
* ongoing navigation prevents repeated administrative breakdown
* financial stabilization restores basic household functioning
* social connection reduces chronic isolation

### Human Condition

**The person regains meaningful ability to continue living, working, caring, deciding, or recovering.**

---

## Level 5 — Structural Capacity Return

### Definition

The system itself changes so that the burden is reduced not only for one person but for others facing the same condition.

Examples:

* childcare capacity expanded
* application process simplified
* transportation gap permanently addressed
* insurance workflow redesigned
* unnecessary documentation removed
* caregiver leave policy improved
* service eligibility broadened
* resource coordination becomes automatic

### Human Condition

**The burden becomes less likely to recur because the surrounding system improved.**

# 6. Capacity Return Is Not Efficiency Alone

A system may become more efficient without returning capacity to the human.

Example:

AI reduces a task from two hours to twenty minutes.

If the organization immediately fills the saved time with additional work, the human may experience:

**Efficiency Gain**

but

**No Capacity Return**

HCOS therefore distinguishes:

## System Efficiency

from

## Human Capacity Return

The question is not only:

**Was time saved?**

It is:

**Who received the benefit of the saved capacity?**

---

# 7. Capacity Capture

HCOS identifies a risk called:

## Capacity Capture

Capacity Capture occurs when a system removes burden but another system immediately absorbs the freed capacity.

Examples:

* automation saves time, then workload increases
* childcare becomes available, but work demands expand
* medical treatment improves, but administrative burden increases
* financial assistance arrives, but fees consume the benefit

Capacity Return should therefore be evaluated over time.

---

# 8. Net Capacity Return

An intervention may reduce one burden while creating another.

HCOS asks:

## What is the net effect?

Example:

**Benefit:** Financial assistance approved

**New burdens:**

* extensive paperwork
* repeated appointments
* transportation costs
* loss of work hours

The intervention may still be useful, but its true capacity return is lower than the assistance amount alone suggests.

---

# 9. Capacity Return Calculation Concept

A qualitative assessment can consider:

**Burden Removed**

minus

**New Burden Created**

plus

**Capacity Restored**

equals

## Net Capacity Return

This is not necessarily intended as a strict numerical formula.

It is a stewardship lens.

---

# 10. Capacity Return Questions

After an intervention, ask:

### Burden

What was the person carrying before?

### Intervention

What changed?

### Access

Was help actually usable?

### New Burden

Did the intervention create additional work, cost, risk, or stress?

### Function

What can the person now do that was previously difficult or impossible?

### Sustainability

Will the benefit continue?

### Structural Learning

Could the underlying system be improved?

# 11. Example — Parent of Child With Cancer

### Initial Condition

Parent is missing work and traveling several hours for treatment.

### Intervention

Transportation support and lodging assistance are arranged.

### Capacity Return

**Financial capacity:** travel costs reduced
**Time capacity:** less time searching for lodging
**Emotional capacity:** greater predictability
**Caregiving capacity:** more attention available for the child

Possible rating:

## Level 3 — Functional Capacity Return

If the support continues throughout treatment:

## Level 4 — Sustained Capacity Return

If the hospital permanently develops a travel-navigation system for all families:

## Level 5 — Structural Capacity Return

# 13. Example — Social Isolation

### Initial Condition

Person has no reliable support network.

### Intervention

System recommends a community group.

### Outcome

Person receives the link but does not attend.

## Level 1 — Informational Return

If the system connects the person directly to a peer navigator:

## Level 2 — Access Return

If an ongoing trusted relationship develops:

## Level 4 — Sustained Capacity Return

# 14. Capacity Return and Human Choice

An intervention should not be rated highly merely because the system believes it was helpful.

Ask:

**Did the human experience the intervention as useful?**

Capacity Return should consider the person's perspective.

A service may appear successful organizationally while creating:

* humiliation
* loss of privacy
* coercion
* dependence
* unwanted intrusion

HCOS requires outcome evaluation from the human perspective.

---

# 15. Capacity Return and Dignity

Relief that comes at the cost of unnecessary loss of dignity should be examined carefully.

Ask:

* Was the person treated respectfully?
* Was unnecessary disclosure required?
* Was help conditional on humiliation?
* Was autonomy preserved?
* Was stigma increased?

Human dignity is part of the outcome.

---

# 16. Capacity Return and Connection

Connection can create capacity even when no immediate material resource changes.

Examples:

* someone finally understands the situation
* a navigator assumes responsibility for coordination
* a trusted peer remains available
* a person no longer has to explain the crisis repeatedly

Connection may return:

* cognitive capacity
* emotional capacity
* time
* confidence
* agency

---

# 17. Capacity Return and Safety

Safety interventions should be evaluated beyond immediate stabilization.

Ask:

**After the immediate danger passed, did the conditions producing the crisis remain unchanged?**

If yes:

the system may have achieved temporary safety without meaningful sustained capacity return.

HCOS therefore distinguishes:

## Immediate Stabilization

from

## Sustainable Relief

Both may be necessary.

---

# 18. Capacity Return Quick Assessment

Rate the intervention:

### Level 0

Nothing became easier.

### Level 1

The person understands more.

### Level 2

Real help becomes accessible.

### Level 3

A concrete burden is reduced.

### Level 4

Meaningful functioning is sustainably restored.

### Level 5

The surrounding system changes.

---

# 19. HCOS Capacity Return Review

Ask:

**What burden existed?**

**What intervention occurred?**

**What changed for the human?**

**What new burden was created?**

**How long will the benefit last?**

**Who captured the benefit?**

**Did the person regain meaningful capacity?**

**Could the system prevent this burden from recurring?**

---

# 21. Platform Use

For social platforms, success should not be measured only through:

* resource clicks
* completed prompts
* engagement
* referrals

Platforms should ask:

**Did the user experience meaningful capacity return?**

This may require:

* voluntary follow-up
* outcome feedback
* privacy-protected measurement
* resource-quality evaluation
* barrier tracking

---

# 22. Structural Learning

Repeated Level 0 or Level 1 outcomes may indicate a larger problem.

Example:

Thousands of people receive housing referrals.

Few obtain housing.

HCOS conclusion:

**The problem may not be insufficient information.**

It may be:

## insufficient housing capacity

Capacity Return measurement helps distinguish:

**information gaps**

from

**resource gaps**

and

**system capacity failures**

---

# 23. Stewardship Standard

A system should not claim success merely because it completed its own process.

Success should be evaluated by the human outcome.

## System completion is not the same as human relief.

---

# 24. Core HCOS Principle

The deepest question is not:

**What did the system do?**

It is:

## What did the human regain?

Time?

Energy?

Safety?

Connection?

Agency?

Money?

Ability to work?

Ability to care?

Ability to recover?

Ability to continue?

That is Capacity Return.
