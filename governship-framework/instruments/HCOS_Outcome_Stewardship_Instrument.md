# HCOS™ Outcome Stewardship Instrument

**Document Type:** Companion Instrument  
**Status:** Version 0.1.0 — Draft  
**Related Framework:** HCOS™ Governship Framework  
**Purpose:** To evaluate whether system measures reflect meaningful outcomes and lead to responsible action.

---

## 1. Purpose

Outcome stewardship ensures that measurement remains connected to the people and human purposes the system exists to serve.

A workflow may be completed, a target may be reached, or a report may be produced without improving care, safety, access, dignity, equity, or human flourishing.

This instrument helps distinguish activity from benefit and measurement from stewardship.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **A measure has limited governship value when no one is responsible for interpreting it, acting upon it, or evaluating whether the response improved the outcome.**

---

## 3. Review Domains

### 3.1 Human Purpose

- What human need or system purpose should the measure represent?
- Who is affected by the outcome?
- Whose experience is absent from the current measurement approach?
- Could organizational performance appear successful while people still experience harm?

### 3.2 Measure Selection

- Is the measure an input, activity, process, output, or outcome?
- Does it reflect meaningful benefit or only completion?
- Is the definition clear and consistently applied?
- Can the measure reveal inequity, exclusion, burden, or unintended harm?
- Are qualitative experiences considered alongside quantitative results?

### 3.3 Evidence and Interpretation

- What evidence supports use of the measure?
- What assumptions are built into its interpretation?
- What limitations, missing data, exclusions, or uncertainty remain?
- Could the measure be gamed or create harmful incentives?
- Who has authority and competence to interpret it?

### 3.4 Action Ownership

- Who reviews the findings?
- Who is responsible for responding?
- What threshold triggers action?
- What authority and resources does the action owner possess?
- How are affected people informed or involved?

### 3.5 Learning and Follow-Through

- Was corrective action implemented?
- Did the action improve the outcome?
- Did it shift burden or create new harm elsewhere?
- What should be revised, preserved, expanded, or retired?
- When will the measure itself be reviewed?

---

## 4. Required Output

Document:

1. Intended human outcome
2. Current measures
3. Gaps between activity and meaningful outcome
4. Populations or perspectives missing
5. Interpretation owner
6. Action owner
7. Trigger thresholds
8. Corrective actions
9. Follow-up measures
10. Review date or trigger

---

## 5. Stewardship Determination

Classify the measurement system as:

- **Descriptive:** Results are collected but do not reliably lead to action.
- **Managed:** Results have owners and defined response pathways.
- **Learning:** Actions are evaluated and measures are revised from experience.
- **Stewarded:** Measurement remains connected to human purpose, equity, action, and long-term learning.

---

## 6. Final Question

> **Are we measuring what matters to people, and are we prepared to act responsibly on what the measures reveal?**
