# HCOS™ Cassandra Reversibility and Risk Guide

**Human-Centered Evaluation of Harm, Error, Vulnerability, and Recoverability**

Framework: **Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)**  
Instrument Family: **HCOS™ Cassandra**

## Purpose

When certainty is incomplete, the system must consider the consequences of both acting and failing to act.

Cassandra evaluates risk through:

**Probability × Severity × Vulnerability × Urgency × Reversibility**

This is a reasoning model rather than a mathematical formula.

---

## 1. False-Negative Risk

A false negative occurs when the system dismisses or underestimates a genuine problem.

Ask:

- What happens if the concern is true and we do nothing?
- Could harm escalate?
- Could evidence disappear?
- Could vulnerable people remain exposed?
- Could psychological or physical harm become difficult to reverse?

---

## 2. False-Positive Risk

A false positive occurs when the system acts as though a conclusion is established when it is not.

Ask:

- Could someone be falsely accused?
- Could relationships be unnecessarily damaged?
- Could someone lose work, reputation, contact, or privacy?
- Could the real cause be overlooked?

---

## 3. Do Not Assume Symmetry

The two errors may have very different consequences.

Temporary distance may be easily reversible.

Continued exposure to serious harm may not be.

Conversely, a public allegation may be extremely difficult to reverse, while continued private investigation may be comparatively low harm.

---

## 4. Vulnerability

Consider children, dependent adults, people with limited mobility or autonomy, people with financial dependence, people experiencing severe distress, and people unable to leave or advocate independently.

Greater vulnerability may justify earlier protection.

---

## 5. Severity

Evaluate potential harm as:

**Low / Moderate / Significant / Severe / Catastrophic**

Consider physical harm, psychological harm, financial harm, privacy loss, educational harm, loss of care, loss of livelihood, reputational harm, and relational harm.

---

## 6. Urgency

Classify:

- Stable.
- Developing.
- Time-sensitive.
- Escalating.
- Immediate.

Urgency affects how long evidence gathering can safely continue before intervention.

---

## 7. Reversibility

Ask:

> **If this decision proves wrong, how easily can we undo its consequences?**

### Highly Reversible

- Seeking perspective.
- Written communication.
- Temporary pause.
- Documentation.

### Moderately Reversible

- Temporary reassignment.
- Reduced contact.
- Temporary supervision changes.

### Difficult to Reverse

- Public accusation.
- Permanent rupture.
- Termination.
- Disclosure of private information.
- Certain legal or institutional consequences.

---

## 8. Least-Irreversible Protection

When uncertainty remains:

> **Prefer the action that reduces serious credible harm while preserving the greatest reasonable ability to revise the decision later.**

---

## 9. Risk Comparison

Ask:

**Risk of waiting:**  
What could happen?

**Risk of acting:**  
What could happen?

**Which consequences are more severe?**

**Which are more reversible?**

**Who bears each risk?**

---

## HCOS™ Reversibility Principle

> **When certainty is limited, reversibility becomes a form of wisdom.**
>
> Protect against serious harm while preserving the ability to learn whenever possible.
