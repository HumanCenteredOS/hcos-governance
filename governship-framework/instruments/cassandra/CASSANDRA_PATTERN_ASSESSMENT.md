# HCOS™ Cassandra Pattern Assessment

**Distinguishing Isolated Events, Recurrence, Escalation, and System Dynamics**

Framework: **Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)**  
Instrument Family: **HCOS™ Cassandra**

## Purpose

One event and a recurring system pattern are not the same.

The Cassandra Pattern Assessment helps determine whether concerning observations appear isolated, recurrent, escalating, cross-contextual, boundary-dependent, or systemic.

---

## 1. Event Level

Ask:

- What happened?
- When?
- Who was involved?
- What preceded it?
- What followed?
- What was the impact?

Do not infer a broad pattern from one event unless that event itself establishes serious danger.

---

## 2. Repetition

Ask:

- Has substantially similar behavior occurred before?
- Does it appear after similar triggers?
- Does the same person repeatedly bear the cost?
- Does the same outcome occur despite different conflicts?

---

## 3. Pattern Components

Look for repetition across:

### Behavior
The same type of action recurs.

### Outcome
Different actions repeatedly produce the same impact.

### Role
The same person repeatedly becomes responsible, blamed, silenced, rescuer, mediator, or problem-holder.

### Trigger
The pattern appears when someone says no, disagrees, leaves, requests privacy, or questions authority.

---

## 4. Escalation

Possible escalation may involve:

- Increasing frequency.
- Greater intensity.
- More people being recruited.
- Stronger threats.
- Greater privacy invasion.
- Increasing financial or relational pressure.
- Movement from verbal conflict toward intimidation or physical danger.

---

## 5. Cross-Setting Pattern

Ask whether similar dynamics appear at home, at work, in caregiving, with extended family, around children, or in institutional interactions.

Cross-setting recurrence can increase confidence that something systematic may be occurring.

---

## 6. Boundary-Response Pattern

Observe what happens when the person attempts to reduce exposure or establish a reasonable limit.

Responses may include:

- Respect.
- Adaptation.
- Negotiation.
- Denial.
- Escalation.
- Guilt.
- Threat.
- Third-party involvement.
- Withdrawal of resources.

---

## 7. Repair Pattern

A healthy repair cycle may involve:

**Harm → Recognition → Accountability → Repair → Change**

An unhealthy recurrence may involve:

**Harm → Crisis → Pressure → Temporary Calm → No Structural Change → Recurrence**

Repeated apology without behavioral change should not automatically be classified as repair.

---

## 8. Pattern Confidence

Classify:

### Isolated
One event with insufficient evidence of recurrence.

### Emerging
Several related observations suggest a possible pattern.

### Recurring
Substantially similar dynamics repeatedly occur.

### Escalating
Frequency, severity, reach, or consequences are increasing.

### Systemic
The pattern is reinforced by multiple relationships, norms, incentives, or structures.

### Unclear
Available evidence does not support confident classification.

---

## 9. Pattern Humility

Ask:

- Could selection bias be occurring?
- Are contradictory events being ignored?
- Is the time frame appropriate?
- Are events genuinely similar?
- Is the same information being counted multiple times?

---

## HCOS™ Pattern Principle

> **Patterns deserve greater attention than isolated incidents, but patterns should emerge from repeated observations—not from repeatedly restating the same interpretation.**
