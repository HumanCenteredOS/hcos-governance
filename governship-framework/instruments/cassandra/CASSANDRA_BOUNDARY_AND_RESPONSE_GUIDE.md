# HCOS™ Cassandra Boundary and Response Guide

**Translating Understanding Into Proportionate Human-Centered Action**

Framework: **Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)**  
Instrument Family: **HCOS™ Cassandra**

## Purpose

Understanding a concern is not enough.

The Cassandra Boundary and Response Guide helps translate assessment into action without requiring unnecessary accusation or premature certainty.

---

## 1. Boundary Versus Judgment

A boundary answers:

> **What will I participate in?**

A judgment answers:

> **What is true about another person?**

A person may establish a boundary without proving the other person's motive or character.

---

## 2. Behavior-Based Boundaries

Prefer:

> “I will end conversations that become insulting.”

Rather than:

> “You are an abusive person.”

The first defines participation.

The second makes a broader conclusion requiring more evidence.

---

## 3. Boundary Components

A clear boundary identifies:

### Behavior
What is happening?

### Limit
What is no longer acceptable?

### Response
What will the person do if it continues?

### Conditions
What would allow future engagement?

---

## 4. Communication Boundaries

Examples:

- Written communication only.
- No third-party messages.
- Scheduled conversations.
- End interaction if yelling begins.
- No discussion of certain private topics.

---

## 5. Privacy Boundaries

Examples:

- Do not share health information.
- Do not distribute messages.
- Do not involve uninvolved relatives.
- Do not discuss children outside legitimate care or safety needs.

---

## 6. Contact Boundaries

Possible responses include:

- Reduced frequency.
- Temporary pause.
- Public-location meetings.
- Third-party presence.
- Supervised contact where appropriate.
- No contact where necessary for safety.

---

## 7. Child and Vulnerable-Person Boundaries

Adults should not ask children or dependents to:

- Carry messages.
- Mediate disputes.
- Choose sides.
- Regulate adult emotions.
- Keep harmful secrets.
- Absorb consequences adults can carry themselves.

---

## 8. Response Escalation

A proportionate sequence may be:

**Clarify → Boundary → Document → Reduce Exposure → Seek Support → Formal Escalation**

Immediate danger may require bypassing intermediate steps.

---

## 9. Boundary Response as New Evidence

Observe whether the boundary produces:

- Respect.
- Negotiation.
- Adaptation.
- Ridicule.
- Threat.
- Escalation.
- Third-party pressure.
- Punishment.

This information should feed back into the Cassandra Loop.

---

## 10. Repair Conditions

Re-engagement may reasonably require:

- Acknowledgment.
- Behavioral change.
- Respect for privacy.
- Reduced coercion.
- Demonstrated reliability.
- Protection of vulnerable people.

Forgiveness should not be treated as a substitute for repair.

---

## HCOS™ Boundary Principle

> **A person does not need complete certainty about another person's motive to decide what conditions are necessary for their own safe participation.**
