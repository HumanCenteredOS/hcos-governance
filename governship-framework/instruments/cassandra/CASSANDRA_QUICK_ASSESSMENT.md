# HCOS™ Cassandra Quick Assessment

**Rapid Human-Centered Evaluation of Warnings, Concerns, and Uncomfortable Information**

Framework: **Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)**
Instrument Family: **HCOS™ Cassandra**
Type: **Rapid Assessment Instrument**

---

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Cassandra Quick Assessment provides a structured first evaluation when someone raises a warning, concern, observation, intuition, or lived experience and the complete truth is not yet known.

It is designed to answer:

> **What do we know, what might be happening, what could be harmed, and what is the safest proportionate action right now?**

The Quick Assessment is not intended to determine guilt, diagnose people, or establish complete causation.

Its purpose is to prevent two common errors:

1. **Premature dismissal**
2. **Premature certainty**

while allowing reasonable protection and further understanding.

---

# 1. State the Concern

Describe the concern in one neutral sentence.

Avoid character judgments or diagnostic labels.

**Concern:**

> ---

Ask:

* What specifically may be happening?
* Who may be affected?
* What makes this concern important now?

---

# 2. Identify Private Reality

What is the person experiencing?

Consider:

* Fear.
* Distress.
* Shame.
* Confusion.
* Hopelessness.
* Loss of functioning.
* Feeling controlled.
* Feeling unsafe.
* Feeling unable to disagree.
* Feeling unable to leave or say no.

Ask:

> **What happens to this person's well-being or functioning around the situation?**

Private reality establishes impact.

It does not automatically establish cause.

---

# 3. Identify Direct Observations

Record what was directly:

* Seen.
* Heard.
* Written.
* Recorded.
* Experienced firsthand.
* Documented.

Do not include assumptions about motive.

Ask:

> **What would a neutral observer be able to describe?**

---

# 4. Separate Observation From Interpretation

For each important piece of information, distinguish:

### Observation

What happened?

### Impact

What happened to the person afterward?

### Interpretation

What might it mean?

Do not convert an interpretation into a fact simply because it appears plausible.

Do not erase severe impact merely because its complete cause remains uncertain.

---

# 5. Consider Alternatives

Ask:

> **What else could reasonably explain what happened?**

Possible contributors may include:

* Misunderstanding.
* Fear.
* Stress.
* Exhaustion.
* Grief.
* Poor communication.
* Different perspectives.
* Limited information.
* Harmful behavior without harmful intent.
* A genuine pattern whose cause is not yet fully understood.

Alternative explanations should expand inquiry.

They should not be used to manufacture doubt or erase credible suffering.

---

# 6. Check for Pattern

Ask:

* Has this happened before?
* Does the same outcome recur?
* Is it becoming more severe?
* Does it happen around the same person or environment?
* Does distress increase when boundaries are attempted?
* Are multiple people observing similar things?
* Are unrelated conflicts repeatedly producing the same relational dynamic?

Classify provisionally as:

**Isolated / Possibly Recurring / Recurring / Escalating / Systemic / Unknown**

---

# 7. Check for Signals Requiring Attention

Pay particular attention to:

* Severe deterioration in functioning.
* Fear of returning to an environment.
* Significant relief after distance.
* Escalating intimidation or aggression.
* Children or vulnerable people carrying adult burdens.
* Repeated privacy violations.
* Isolation.
* Threats involving housing, money, custody, employment, belonging, or safety.
* Repeated third-party pressure.
* Profound hopelessness associated with a particular environment.

These signals do not establish complete causation.

They may increase the need for protection.

---

# 8. Examine Power and Pressure

Ask:

### Messenger

What could this person lose by speaking?

### Listener

What becomes difficult if the concern is true?

### System

Who benefits if the concern disappears?

Then ask:

> **Who currently has the greatest ability to impose consequences on someone else?**

Power does not determine truth.

Power affects risk.

---

# 9. Assess Risk

## If the concern is substantially true:

What could happen if nothing changes?

Consider:

* Severity.
* Vulnerability.
* Escalation.
* Duration.
* Irreversibility.

## If the concern is false or incomplete:

What harm could occur from acting too strongly?

Consider:

* False accusation.
* Relationship damage.
* Unnecessary restriction.
* Privacy loss.
* Reputational harm.
* Missing the actual cause.

Do not assume the two risks are equal.

---

# 10. Assess Reversibility

Ask:

> **Which mistake would be hardest to undo?**

Prefer protective actions that:

* Reduce serious risk.
* Preserve dignity.
* Avoid unnecessary accusation.
* Can be revised as evidence develops.

---

# 11. Determine the Protection Threshold

Ask which response is justified now:

### Listen

The concern deserves respectful attention.

### Clarify

More information is needed.

### Document

The concern or impact should be tracked.

### Investigate

There is enough concern to seek additional evidence.

### Protect

Credible risk justifies temporary protective action.

### Escalate

Significant or immediate danger requires appropriate professional, safeguarding, legal, medical, or emergency response.

Protection does not require pretending that every interpretation has been proven.

---

# 12. Identify the Safest Proportionate Action

Ask:

> **What action would reduce meaningful risk today without creating unnecessary irreversible harm?**

Possible actions may include:

* Pause.
* Gather information.
* Document.
* Move communication to writing.
* Establish a privacy boundary.
* Reduce exposure.
* Seek independent perspective.
* Create temporary distance.
* Increase support.
* Protect a vulnerable person.
* Use appropriate professional channels.

# 15. Final Cassandra Check

Ask:

> **If the warning is true, what would we regret failing to do today?**

> **If the warning is false or incomplete, what would we regret doing prematurely?**

> **What is the safest proportionate action available now?**

And finally:

> **Are we seeking understanding—or merely trying to make the concern disappear?**

## Quick Assessment Outcome

**Current concern level:**
Low / Moderate / Significant / High / Immediate

**Evidence strength:**
Limited / Emerging / Moderate / Strong / Unknown

**Potential harm:**
Low / Moderate / Significant / Severe

**Reversibility of protective action:**
High / Moderate / Low

**Recommended response:**
Observe / Clarify / Document / Investigate / Protect / Escalate

**Reassessment needed:**
Yes / No

---

## HCOS™ Cassandra Principle

> **Uncertainty is not proof.**
>
> **Uncertainty is also not permission to ignore credible harm.**
>
> Protect proportionately while understanding grows.
