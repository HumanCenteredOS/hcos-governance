# HCOS™ Cassandra Information Stewardship

**Human-Centered Stewardship of Privacy, Evidence, Narrative, and Disclosure**

Framework: **Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)**  
Instrument Family: **HCOS™ Cassandra**

## Purpose

Information can protect people.

Information can also be used to shame, pressure, expose, discredit, isolate, or control them.

Cassandra therefore treats information as something to **steward**, not something to win with.

---

## 1. Core Questions

Before sharing information, ask:

- Is sharing necessary?
- Is consent available?
- Who has a legitimate role?
- How much information is actually needed?
- What harm could disclosure create?
- Will sharing improve understanding or merely widen conflict?
- Could the information be used against the person later?

---

## 2. Support-Seeking

Appropriate support-seeking generally involves:

- A legitimate need for help.
- Limited disclosure.
- Appropriate audience.
- Respect for privacy.
- Focus on understanding or safety.

---

## 3. Exposure

Exposure occurs when private information is shared unnecessarily or beyond legitimate need.

Potential consequences include:

- Loss of dignity.
- Reduced trust.
- Social pressure.
- Reputation damage.
- Loss of control over one's own story.

---

## 4. Triangulation

Triangulation occurs when third parties are brought into a disagreement instead of communication occurring appropriately between those involved.

Not every third-party involvement is harmful.

Mediators, clinicians, advocates, legal counsel, or safety professionals may have legitimate roles.

The question is:

> **Is the third party helping resolve or understand the concern, or being used to pressure someone?**

---

## 5. Coalition-Building

Coalition-building occurs when multiple people are recruited to strengthen one narrative or compel another person to comply.

Look for:

- Repeated coordinated contacts.
- Pressure to apologize.
- Pressure to return.
- Shared talking points.
- One-sided information.
- Lack of independent inquiry.

---

## 6. Narrative Control

Narrative control may occur when one person or group determines what information others hear, which context is included, which information is withheld, who gets to speak, and whose interpretation becomes accepted reality.

---

## 7. Credibility Undermining

Personal information should not be used reflexively to invalidate a concern.

Examples may include invoking:

- Medical history.
- Mental health history.
- Past mistakes.
- Emotional reactions.
- Financial dependence.
- Previous conflict.

These facts may sometimes be relevant.

They should not substitute for evaluating the concern itself.

---

## 8. Proportional Disclosure

Share the least amount of information reasonably necessary for:

- Protection.
- Care.
- Legitimate consultation.
- Legal guidance.
- Formal investigation.
- Accountability.

More disclosure is not automatically better disclosure.

---

## 9. Evidence Preservation

When appropriate:

- Preserve original messages.
- Record dates.
- Maintain original files.
- Keep factual notes separate from interpretation.
- Avoid editing evidence.
- Avoid unnecessary circulation.

---

## 10. Information Propagation Map

Ask:

**Source → Recipient → Additional Recipient → Result**

This can reveal whether one story became socially amplified through repeated sharing.

---

## 11. Audience Test

Before disclosure, ask:

- Can this person help?
- Will they protect privacy?
- Are they independent?
- Do they need this information?
- Are they likely to escalate?
- Can they tolerate uncertainty?
- Will disclosure make the person safer?

---

## 12. Competing Campaigns

Do not respond to one coalition by constructing another simply to overpower it.

Independent, thoughtful support is preferable to social warfare.

---

## 13. Right to One's Own Story

Human-centered information stewardship recognizes that people should retain meaningful agency over personal information whenever safety and legitimate obligations allow.

---

## HCOS™ Information Principle

> **Information should move according to legitimate need, not curiosity, power, fear, or social pressure.**
>
> The purpose of evidence is understanding and protection—not domination.
