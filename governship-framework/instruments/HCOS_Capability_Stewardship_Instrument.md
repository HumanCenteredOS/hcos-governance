# HCOS™ Capability Stewardship Instrument

**Document Type:** Companion Instrument  
**Status:** Version 0.1.0 — Draft  
**Related Framework:** HCOS™ Governship Framework  
**Purpose:** To evaluate whether the people, culture, knowledge, research process, and organizational supports can sustain a method or workflow responsibly.

---

## 1. Purpose

Capability stewardship recognizes that a method or workflow cannot remain healthy when the surrounding human system lacks the capacity to operate, question, review, and improve it.

The existence of a policy, tool, workflow, or human-review step does not prove that the organization has the capability required to use it responsibly.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **A workflow is only as sustainable as the human and organizational capability surrounding it.**

---

## 3. Review Domains

### 3.1 Workforce Capacity

- Are staffing, time, workload, and recovery conditions adequate?
- Do responsibilities match available authority and resources?
- Are critical roles dependent on one person?
- Can the workforce absorb exceptions, incidents, and change?

### 3.2 Knowledge and Competence

- Do people understand the method, evidence, limitations, and escalation pathways?
- Is training role-specific and current?
- Is protected learning time available?
- Can people recognize when the workflow no longer fits the situation?

### 3.3 Culture and Questioning Protection

- Can people question assumptions or raise concerns without retaliation?
- Are dissent and frontline observations treated as useful system information?
- Can work be paused when harm is possible?
- Are leaders accountable for responding to concerns?

### 3.4 Research and Evidence Capability

- Can the organization find, appraise, document, and update evidence?
- Are methods appropriate to the claims being made?
- Are uncertainty and limitations visible?
- Can new evidence trigger review and revision?

### 3.5 Technical and Operational Support

- Are tools, data, documentation, access, maintenance, and technical support sufficient?
- Are failures and workarounds visible?
- Is human review meaningful or symbolic?
- Can reviewers access sources and reverse decisions?

### 3.6 Continuity and Institutional Memory

- What knowledge would be lost if key contributors left?
- Are decisions, assumptions, and revisions documented?
- Are succession and knowledge-transfer plans defined?
- Who maintains the capability over time?

---

## 4. Required Output

Document:

1. Required capabilities
2. Current strengths
3. Capacity gaps
4. Questioning and escalation protections
5. Single-person dependencies
6. Research and evidence gaps
7. Technical and operational gaps
8. Training and support plan
9. Continuity and succession plan
10. Responsible owners and review dates

---

## 5. Capability Determination

Classify capability as:

- **Fragile:** Depends on individual effort, memory, or informal workarounds.
- **Defined:** Required roles and knowledge are identified but inconsistently supported.
- **Sustained:** Staffing, training, support, questioning, and continuity are operationalized.
- **Stewarded:** Capability is monitored, renewed, protected, and adapted as the system changes.

---

## 6. Final Question

> **Does the organization possess—and protect—the human and institutional capability required to carry this responsibility over time?**
