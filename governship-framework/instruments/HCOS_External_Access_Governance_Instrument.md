# HCOS™ External-Access Governance Instrument

**Document Type:** Companion Instrument  
**Status:** Version 0.1.0 — Draft  
**Related Framework:** HCOS™ Governship Framework  
**Purpose:** To evaluate access, exclusion, information sharing, reimbursement, and accountability across organizational boundaries.

---

## 1. Purpose

External-access governance addresses the responsibilities that arise when authority, services, data, reimbursement, distribution, referrals, or care move between organizations.

Fragmented systems can create situations in which each organization controls one part of the process while no one remains visibly accountable for the whole human outcome.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **When authority, information, or work crosses an organizational boundary, accountability must remain visible rather than disappearing between institutions.**

---

## 3. Review Domains

### 3.1 Access and Inclusion

- Who controls entry into the network, service, workflow, or distribution channel?
- What criteria determine inclusion or exclusion?
- Are criteria transparent, evidence-informed, and consistently applied?
- Who is disproportionately excluded or burdened?
- Is there an appeal or exception pathway?

### 3.2 Authority and Responsibility

- What authority does each organization hold?
- Which decisions can each party make?
- Who owns each handoff, delay, denial, restriction, or escalation?
- Has responsibility been transferred without sufficient authority or resources?
- Who is accountable for the combined outcome?

### 3.3 Data and Information Sharing

- Who owns or stewards the data?
- Who may access, use, transfer, retain, correct, or delete it?
- Are consent, privacy, confidentiality, and security obligations clear?
- Is necessary context lost during transfer?
- How are errors corrected across systems?

### 3.4 Reimbursement and Incentives

- How do payment, coverage, contracting, limited distribution, or network incentives shape decisions?
- Could financial incentives delay, deny, redirect, or fragment access?
- Are incentive conflicts visible?
- Who bears the financial and human cost of failure?

### 3.5 Coordination and Escalation

- Are communication and handoff expectations defined?
- How are disputes handled?
- Can affected people identify the responsible party?
- Is there a cross-organizational escalation pathway?
- How are recurring failures connected and reviewed?

---

## 4. Required Output

Create:

1. External-party map
2. Access and exclusion criteria
3. Decision-rights matrix
4. Data-sharing responsibility map
5. Reimbursement and incentive assessment
6. Handoff and escalation pathway
7. Human-impact assessment
8. Accountability gaps
9. Corrective actions and owners
10. Review date or trigger

---

## 5. Governship Warning Signs

- No organization owns the whole outcome.
- Each party attributes failure to another party.
- Access criteria are opaque or inconsistently applied.
- Data move without sufficient context or correction pathways.
- Reimbursement incentives override human need.
- People must coordinate the organizations themselves.
- Appeals stop at organizational boundaries.
- Vendors or partners exercise authority without equivalent accountability.

---

## 6. Final Question

> **Can a person understand who controls access, who holds responsibility, and where accountability remains when the work crosses organizational boundaries?**
