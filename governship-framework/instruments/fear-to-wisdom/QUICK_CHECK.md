# HCOS™ Fear-to-Wisdom Quick Check

**Version:** 1.5 Draft

This quick check is designed for everyday use when a person, family, team, organization, institution, or system is responding to uncertainty, risk, conflict, suffering, or change.

## 12-Question Quick Check

1. **Signal** — What is happening, and what is fear telling us?
2. **Love** — Who and what matters enough to protect?
3. **Dignity** — Is every human being treated as fully human?
4. **Autonomy** — Who should have voice, boundaries, participation, consent, choice, and authority?
5. **Truth & Perspective** — What do we know, what are we assuming, and what can different people see?
6. **Wisdom** — What is the least harmful, proportionate, reversible response?
7. **Capacity** — What can the humans involved realistically carry?
8. **Action** — What support, protection, information, resource, connection, or intervention is needed?
9. **Functional Autonomy** — Are the choices understandable, accessible, viable, and meaningfully refuseable?
10. **Pathway** — Can the action actually connect to access, opportunity, and usable reality?
11. **Power & Stewardship** — Who controls the decision, resources, consequences, burden, benefit, and ability to refuse?
12. **Learning & Transformation** — What needs to be repaired, restored, learned, or structurally changed?

---

## Final Human-Centered Test

Before concluding an HCOS evaluation, ask:

- Are we protecting humans—or merely controlling uncertainty?
- Are we listening—or merely seeking compliance?
- Is fear narrowing perspective?
- Was the human merely permitted to speak, or could their voice meaningfully enter the decision?
- Are we supporting agency—or creating unnecessary dependency?
- Is consent meaningful—or are silence, compliance, dependency, or fear being mistaken for consent?
- Could the human meaningfully say no?
- If they said no, what would happen?
- Does the person exercising authority actually possess legitimate authority over this decision?
- Is that authority being exercised within appropriate boundaries?
- Is intervention necessary?
- Is it proportionate?
- Is it the least restrictive workable response?
- Are children being heard without being required to carry adult responsibility?
- Is reduced capacity being mistaken for absence of autonomy?
- Are restrictions still necessary—or have yesterday's protections become today's controls?
- Are theoretical choices actually viable choices?
- Are we assigning responsibility where meaningful control actually resides?
- Are we asking a suffering human to become more resilient when the pathway itself needs repair?
- Are hope, faith, or spirituality creating possibility—or being transformed into blame when outcomes remain outside human control?
- Is power increasing protection, capacity, understanding, participation, and autonomy—or increasing dependence upon those who possess power?
- What did the outcome teach us?
- What harm requires repair?
- What structural condition allowed unnecessary suffering to persist?

And finally:

> **What is the smallest connection, clarification, resource, support, protection, restoration of autonomy, removal of unnecessary control, or structural change that could reopen meaningful possibility?**
