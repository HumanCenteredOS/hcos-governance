# Purpose

The HCOS™ Governship Glossary establishes common terminology used throughout the Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™) ecosystem.

A shared vocabulary promotes consistency across HCOS foundations, disciplines, standards, methods, models, instruments, governance documents, educational materials, and future implementations.

Unless otherwise specified, these definitions apply throughout the HCOS framework.

---

# Guiding Principle

Healthy knowledge systems use language consistently.

Shared definitions reduce misunderstanding, strengthen collaboration, improve transparency, and preserve meaning over time.

---

# A

## Accountability

The responsibility to answer for decisions, actions, and outcomes within the scope of one's authority, role, and responsibilities.

Healthy accountability aligns responsibility with actual authority, available information, resources, and capacity.

Accountability should not assign responsibility where meaningful authority did not exist.

---

## Analysis

The systematic effort to understand why something happened.

Analysis seeks explanation rather than blame by examining system conditions, human factors, workflows, technology, policies, resources, evidence, and uncertainty.

Analysis answers:

> Why did this happen?

---

## Architecture

The intentional organization of HCOS resources, relationships, boundaries, and structures that together create a coherent knowledge ecosystem.

---

## Authority

The legitimate ability to make decisions, allocate resources, establish direction, or approve actions within defined responsibilities.

Authority should always be matched with accountability.

---

# C

## Change Log

A documented history of meaningful revisions made to an HCOS resource.

Change logs improve transparency, traceability, and historical understanding.

---

## Continuous Learning

The ongoing process of improving HCOS through experience, evidence, reflection, feedback, research, and responsible revision.

Learning is viewed as a strength rather than evidence of previous failure.

---

# D

## Decision Owner

The individual or designated role with authority to approve, reject, revise, suspend, or retire a resource or decision.

---

## Discipline

A major domain of HCOS knowledge organized around a particular area of practice or inquiry.

Examples include Human Systems, Human-Centered AI, Healthcare Systems, and Human Load Protection.

---

## Document Lifecycle

The progression of an HCOS resource from initial concept through development, review, publication, revision, archival, or retirement.

---

# E

## Evidence

Information used to support understanding, decisions, or claims.

Evidence may include research, operational experience, expert review, implementation findings, or other documented sources.

HCOS distinguishes evidence from interpretation, professional judgment, assumptions, and future aspirations.

---

## Ecosystem

The complete collection of interconnected HCOS repositories, resources, disciplines, governance processes, contributors, and implementations.

---

# G

## Governance

The structures, processes, responsibilities, and oversight that protect the integrity of the present system.

Governance defines authority, accountability, review, versioning, quality expectations, architectural boundaries, and change management.

Governance protects today's HCOS.

---

## Governship

The integrated practice of governing through stewardship.

Governship unites governance and stewardship by balancing authority with humility, accountability with compassion, structure with adaptation, and present responsibility with future care.

Governship protects both institutional integrity and human purpose.

---

# H

## Human Dignity

The inherent worth possessed by every person.

Within HCOS, dignity is not earned through productivity, status, performance, or institutional value.

Healthy systems recognize, respect, and protect human dignity in every decision.

---

## Human Flourishing

The long-term condition in which individuals, organizations, and communities are able to grow, contribute, adapt, learn, and thrive while preserving dignity, purpose, and meaningful relationships.

---

# I

## Implementation

The practical application of HCOS resources within real organizations, technologies, communities, or systems.

Implementation translates knowledge into practice.

---

## Instrument

A structured HCOS resource designed to assess, measure, facilitate discussion, support reflection, or guide practical application.

---

# K

## Knowledge Stewardship

The responsible preservation, development, organization, validation, revision, and retirement of knowledge over time.

Knowledge stewardship seeks to ensure that future users inherit resources that remain trustworthy, understandable, and useful.

---

# M

## Method

A repeatable approach for accomplishing a particular task within HCOS.

Methods describe how work is performed.

---

## Model

A conceptual representation used to explain relationships, organize thinking, or improve understanding.

Models help people understand systems without attempting to reproduce every detail.

---

# P

## Principle

A foundational statement that guides decisions and behavior across the HCOS ecosystem.

Principles are intended to remain relatively stable over time.

---

## Prompt

A structured set of instructions intended to guide interaction with an AI system toward a defined purpose.

HCOS prompts should remain transparent, human-centered, and appropriately scoped.

---

# R

## Repository

A structured collection of HCOS resources organized around a defined purpose within the overall ecosystem.

---

## Review

The intentional evaluation of a resource to assess quality, coherence, evidence, usability, alignment, and readiness for its intended purpose.

Review should be proportional to potential impact and risk.

---

# S

## Standard

An HCOS document describing expected practices, principles, requirements, or guidance for a defined area of work.

Standards promote consistency while allowing thoughtful adaptation to context.

---

## Steward

A person or role responsible for preserving the long-term integrity, usefulness, continuity, and purpose of an HCOS resource or system.

---

## Stewardship

The responsible care, preservation, adaptation, and improvement of knowledge, systems, relationships, and resources over time.

Stewardship protects tomorrow's HCOS by supporting continuous learning, responsible evolution, preservation of meaning, and future generations.

---

## System

A collection of interconnected people, processes, technologies, resources, relationships, and structures working together toward a shared purpose.

Within HCOS, every organization is understood as a human system.

---

# T

## Traceability

The ability to understand what changed, why it changed, who approved it, what evidence informed the decision, and how it relates to previous versions.

Traceability supports transparency, learning, and accountability.

---

## Transparency

The practice of making meaningful decisions, assumptions, limitations, evidence, and changes understandable to others.

Transparency promotes trust without requiring disclosure of protected or confidential information.

---

# V

## Validation

The process of gathering evidence to determine whether an HCOS resource performs its intended purpose within its intended context.

Validation may involve expert review, implementation experience, user feedback, operational testing, or research.

Validation is an ongoing activity rather than a single event.

---

## Version

A documented release of an HCOS resource identified through semantic versioning.

Version numbers communicate the maturity and evolution of a resource over time.

---

# W

## Working Draft

A resource that is actively being developed and should not yet be interpreted as fully reviewed, validated, or authoritative.

Working drafts exist to encourage thoughtful refinement before publication.

---

# Relationship to the HCOS™ Governship Framework

This glossary supports consistent interpretation of terminology used throughout the HCOS™ Governship Framework and related governance documents.

Definitions may evolve as the HCOS ecosystem grows.

Meaningful revisions should be documented through normal HCOS review, versioning, and change management processes.

---

# Guiding Principle

Language shapes understanding.

Shared understanding strengthens stewardship.

Healthy stewardship preserves meaning so that future generations inherit knowledge that remains coherent, trustworthy, and useful.
